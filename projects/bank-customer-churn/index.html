Project Overview

Research Objective

Develop classification models to predict customer churn in the banking sector, enabling early identification of potential churners and supporting data-driven customer retention strategies.

Methodology

The study compares multiple supervised learning algorithms including Naive Bayes, Decision Tree, K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), with additional techniques such as feature scaling, SMOTE, and hyperparameter tuning.

Key Findings

Model Performance

MLP Performs Best

The Multilayer Perceptron achieved the highest classification accuracy and ROC-AUC score, outperforming traditional models such as Naive Bayes and Decision Tree.

Data Characteristics

Class Imbalance Issue

The dataset exhibits class imbalance between churned and retained customers. Applying SMOTE significantly improved recall and overall model robustness.

Business Insight

Retention Strategy

Customers with low tenure, high service usage, and declining balances are more likely to churn, highlighting the importance of personalized retention programs.
